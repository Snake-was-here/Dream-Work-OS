# Matching

Match the **actual task** to the profile; keywords are search aids, not proof. Formal education is a gap only when mandatory or materially necessary to do the work. Separate achievable work from unverified claims of prior experience.

## Score (0–100)

| Dimension | Points | Assessment |
|---|---:|---|
| Problem fit | 0–30 | Could the owner's confirmed skills plausibly solve the concrete problem? |
| Demonstrated proof | 0–25 | Relevant approved portfolio/CV evidence; self-report alone earns 0–5 |
| Learning feasibility | 0–15 | Missing skills, timeline and scope; unknown requirements lower confidence |
| Current demand / route | 0–15 | Verified active need and legitimate reachable application/contact |
| Practical terms | 0–10 | Pay, location, eligibility, hours; unknown preferences are unresolved |
| Relationship / experience value | 0–5 | Concrete potential value, avoiding speculative prestige |

The score is a prioritization aid, not a hiring probability. Explain the components and confidence in Notes. Initial profiles without proof normally have **provisional** matches; avoid a high-confidence label. Do not give points for invented projects or undisclosed compensation. Prefer paid work; unpaid or unclear commercial terms need a visible caveat and human decision.

## Required row assessment
- **Why I Match:** 1–3 sentences connecting the problem to confirmed skills and any approved evidence. Label transferability as inference.
- **Relevant Portfolio Work:** Approved project IDs/links, or “None verified”.
- **Potential Gaps:** Specific missing proof, experience, technologies, availability, eligibility or terms.
- **Evidence:** Source excerpt/paraphrase, posting/verification dates, active/filled signals and URL. Keep quotes brief.

Hard blockers (filled/expired listing, DO NOT CONTACT, explicit incompatible eligibility) prevent outreach regardless of score. Unknown terms remain visible rather than assumed compatible. Re-score after new evidence; preserve the owner's decision and log meaningful reasoning changes. Learn from approvals and measured outcomes, not from a desire to inflate scores.
